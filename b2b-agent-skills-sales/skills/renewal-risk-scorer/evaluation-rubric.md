# Renewal Risk Scorer — Evaluation Rubric

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Risk Score Accuracy & Justification (20 points)

**Excellent (18-20):**
- Risk score is mathematically grounded in at least 5 specific data points
- High-risk, medium-risk, and protective signals are clearly identified and weighted
- Score reflects appropriate balance between usage metrics, engagement, and commercial signals
- Score is defensible to executive audience; could be cited in a forecast meeting

**Good (15-17):**
- Risk score is based on 3-4 key data points
- Signal weighting is reasonable but not fully explicit
- Score feels directionally correct but could use more precision
- Would hold up to basic questioning

**Adequate (12-14):**
- Risk score is present but justification is thin
- Some signals are weighted but not systematically
- Score feels right intuitively but lacks rigor
- May not hold up to detailed scrutiny

**Poor (0-11):**
- Risk score lacks clear justification
- Signal weighting is inconsistent or missing
- Score feels arbitrary or unsupported by data

### 2. Risk Driver Quality & Specificity (20 points)

**Excellent (18-20):**
- Top 3-5 risk drivers are distinct and non-overlapping
- Each driver is tied to specific account data (e.g., "usage down 22% over 90 days" vs. "usage declining")
- Driver implications are specific to this account's context and business model
- Drivers rank clearly in order of impact
- Could be cited directly in a business review or renewal proposal

**Good (15-17):**
- Risk drivers are distinct and mostly specific
- Most drivers tie back to account data
- Implications are relevant but could be more specific to this account
- Ranking is reasonable
- Usable in a renewal conversation with some additional context

**Adequate (12-14):**
- Risk drivers are identified but may be generic or overlapping
- Data connection is partial or indirect
- Implications are relevant but somewhat generic
- Ranking could be clearer
- Requires interpretation to use in a business context

**Poor (0-11):**
- Risk drivers are vague or generic
- Limited connection to actual account data
- Implications are unclear or irrelevant
- Not usable in a real renewal scenario

### 3. Intervention Recommendations — Specificity & Executability (20 points)

**Excellent (18-20):**
- Interventions are organized into immediate, short-term, and strategic tiers
- Each action has a specific owner and timeline
- Success criteria are measurable or clearly defined
- Recommendations feel targeted to this account's specific risks
- An account manager could pick up this plan and execute it immediately
- Addresses root causes, not just symptoms

**Good (15-17):**
- Interventions are organized by timeframe
- Most actions have owners and timelines
- Some success criteria are clear; others are implied
- Recommendations are mostly specific to this account
- Usable with minor clarification or adaptation
- Addresses some root causes

**Adequate (12-14):**
- Interventions are present but organization could be clearer
- Not all actions have clear owners or timelines
- Success criteria are vague
- Some recommendations are generic rather than account-specific
- Requires interpretation and adaptation to be executed
- Addresses symptoms more than root causes

**Poor (0-11):**
- Interventions are vague or generic
- No clear owners or timelines
- No clear success criteria
- Not specific to this account
- Not actionable without significant reinterpretation

### 4. Business Context & Strategic Framing (15 points)

**Excellent (14-15):**
- Output shows understanding of this account's industry, business model, or strategic priorities
- Recognizes critical inflection points (e.g., CTO transition, tool consolidation, budget cycle)
- Frames renewal in terms of strategic value, not just cost preservation
- Shows awareness of stakeholder incentives and power dynamics
- Helps the account team think differently about the renewal

**Good (12-13):**
- Output shows awareness of key account dynamics
- Recognizes important context (e.g., organizational changes, budget cycles)
- Framing is strategic in most places
- Shows some stakeholder awareness
- Provides useful perspective on the renewal

**Adequate (10-11):**
- Output shows basic account awareness
- Limited recognition of strategic context
- Framing is mostly tactical or generic
- Some stakeholder awareness
- Helpful but could go deeper

**Poor (0-9):**
- Output misses account context
- No strategic framing
- Little to no stakeholder awareness
- Reads as template-like or generic

### 5. Renewal Probability Estimate — Realism & Consistency (15 points)

**Excellent (14-15):**
- Probability estimate is consistent with risk score and evidence
- Provides differentiated scenarios (base case, upside with intervention, downside)
- Estimate is realistic for a B2B SaaS account (not binary, accounts for execution uncertainty)
- Shows understanding of what moves the needle between scenarios
- Ties directly back to risk drivers

**Good (12-13):**
- Probability estimate is mostly consistent with risk score
- Single scenario or two scenarios provided
- Estimate is realistic
- Shows some understanding of what improves outcomes
- Generally aligned with risk assessment

**Adequate (10-11):**
- Probability estimate is somewhat consistent with risk score
- Single scenario or generic ranges
- Estimate feels approximately right but could be more rigorous
- Shows basic understanding of levers
- Loosely connected to drivers

**Poor (0-9):**
- Probability estimate is disconnected from risk score or evidence
- Unrealistic (e.g., binary churn/no-churn, overly confident)
- No clear basis or logic
- Little to no connection to drivers

### 6. Format & Clarity (10 points)

**Excellent (9-10):**
- Output follows the specified template closely
- All required sections are present and complete
- Content is well-organized and easy to scan
- Language is professional and clear
- No ambiguous statements

**Good (8):**
- Output follows template with minor deviations
- All required sections present
- Organization is clear
- Language is professional
- Minor areas of ambiguity

**Adequate (6-7):**
- Output follows template loosely
- Some sections may be missing or incomplete
- Organization could be clearer
- Language is clear but could be more concise
- Some ambiguity

**Poor (0-5):**
- Output deviates significantly from template
- Missing sections or incomplete sections
- Disorganized or hard to follow
- Unclear or verbose language
- Significant ambiguity

## Overall Scoring

**Excellent (90-100):**
- Risk score is rigorous and justified
- Risk drivers are specific and actionable
- Interventions are immediately executable
- Shows strong business context and strategic framing
- Probability estimate is realistic and internally consistent
- Format is professional and complete

**Good (80-89):**
- Risk score is sound and mostly justified
- Risk drivers are mostly specific
- Interventions are mostly actionable
- Shows reasonable business context
- Probability estimate is realistic
- Format is mostly complete

**Adequate (70-79):**
- Risk score is present and reasonable
- Risk drivers are identified but could be more specific
- Interventions require some interpretation
- Shows basic business context
- Probability estimate is reasonable
- Format is generally complete

**Needs Improvement (Below 70):**
- Risk score lacks rigor or clarity
- Risk drivers are vague or generic
- Interventions are not actionable
- Limited business context
- Probability estimate is unclear or unrealistic
- Format is incomplete or unclear

## Key Evaluation Priorities

1. **Specificity over generic language:** A good assessment should feel like it's written for this specific account, not a template applied to 10 accounts.

2. **Actionability:** An intervention that can't be assigned to a person and executed is not useful. Vague recommendations should downgrade the score significantly.

3. **Justification:** A risk score or probability estimate without clear justification is unreliable. Look for evidence, not assertion.

4. **Business acumen:** The best assessments show understanding of the account's strategic context, not just data points. Does the output help the team think differently about the renewal?

5. **Realism:** Probability estimates should avoid binary thinking. Real renewals are uncertain and contingent on execution.

## Usage Notes

- Use this rubric to calibrate quality across multiple renewal risk assessments
- High-scoring assessments can serve as templates for team members
- Scores below 75 should prompt revision or additional data gathering
- This rubric is diagnostic: it helps teams understand what's missing from a renewal risk assessment
