# Account Plan Generator

## Why this skill exists

Many strategic accounts fail not because the product is wrong, but because the sales team does not have a shared operating plan.

Without a clear account plan, sellers often rely on scattered notes, incomplete stakeholder understanding, and vague next steps. That creates inconsistent execution and slow momentum.

This skill helps turn a strategic account into a working plan with clear priorities, decision context, risk awareness, and action sequencing.

## Expert memory layer

A strong account plan is not a generic summary. It should capture patterns that experienced account teams see repeatedly:

- which stakeholders actually influence the decision
- where buying consensus is weak or fragmented
- what risks are likely to delay the deal
- which business outcomes matter to the customer
- what actions create momentum versus wasted activity

This is the memory layer that makes account planning useful in real enterprise sales motions.

## Business objective

This skill helps the team answer:

> What should we do next to move this account forward, and how do we align the broader buying group around a realistic path?

## Inputs

- account summary and account strategy overview
- goals or business outcomes the customer wants to solve
- stakeholder map and buying group context
- opportunity stage and deal status
- competitive context and recent buying signals
- success criteria, blockers, and constraints

## Decision logic

A useful account plan should balance four things:

1. customer priorities
2. buying process and stakeholder complexity
3. commercial and strategic value
4. operational risk and execution readiness

The best plans do not just list goals. They connect the account's business reality to a sequence of actions the team can execute.

## Common failure patterns

This skill should guard against weak reasoning such as:

- treating all stakeholders as equal decision-makers
- ignoring buying committee complexity
- focusing only on product fit without business context
- creating plans that are too broad to execute
- failing to identify the real blockers or dependencies

## Outputs

A strong account plan should include:

- account objective and strategic intent
- key stakeholder map and influence patterns
- buying criteria and likely decision process
- known risks, blockers, and dependencies
- recommended actions in priority order
- milestones or executive checkpoints

## Example result

### Strategic account plan: Account X
- Goal: reduce operational inefficiency and improve visibility across the revenue workflow
- Key stakeholders: VP of operations, finance lead, IT leadership, customer success sponsor
- Buying criteria: measurable operational efficiency, implementation speed, minimal business disruption
- Risk: lack of executive alignment and unclear cross-functional ownership
- Recommended actions:
  1. align stakeholder priorities and create a cross-functional success map
  2. validate business case with finance and operations leaders
  3. secure executive sponsor for rollout readiness
  4. define a phased implementation roadmap with measurable success checkpoints

## Recommended prompt

> You are a strategic B2B account planning expert. Build a practical account plan for the target account using the context provided. Include business goals, buying process details, stakeholders and influence patterns, likely risks or blockers, and a prioritized sequence of actions that would move the account forward. Focus on what the sales team should do next and how to align the buying group around realistic execution.
