# Market Shift Monitor

## Why this skill exists

Market change is usually noisy. Most teams do not need more signals. They need a disciplined way to understand which signals matter and which ones are just background noise.

This skill helps GTM teams detect real market movement, competitor pressure, and buyer behavior change before they become strategic surprises.

It is built to separate meaningful shifts from generic chatter and then translate those shifts into practical Go-to-Market implications.

## Expert memory layer

Experienced GTM teams build memory around patterns such as:

- when competitor movement is actually changing buying behavior
- which buyer signals indicate urgency vs noise
- which market changes require repositioning versus a tactical response
- which category shifts deserve product, messaging, or pricing attention

This skill turns those patterns into a structured monitoring and response workflow.

## Business objective

This skill helps the team answer:

> Which market or competitive signals are materially changing buyer behavior, and what should we do about it?

## Inputs

- market trend reports and category signals
- competitor announcements and product updates
- customer conversation themes and buying concerns
- pricing, packaging, or positioning changes
- campaign and intent data
- segment-specific demand patterns

## Decision logic

A useful market monitor should assess:

1. signal strength: how large or credible is the change?
2. buyer relevance: does it affect buying criteria or urgency?
3. strategic impact: does it require messaging, targeting, or positioning change?
4. response urgency: is this a near-term action or a watch item?

The most important question is not “what changed?” but “what does this change mean for our GTM motion?”

## Common failure patterns

This skill should guard against weak reasoning such as:

- responding to every competitor move as if it were strategic
- treating category noise as real market change
- ignoring customer language and buying behavior
- overlooking what the signal means for pricing, messaging, or segmentation
- reacting without understanding whether the shift is temporary or structural

## Outputs

A useful output should include:

- market shift summary
- competitor movement summary
- likely buyer impact
- segment or persona relevance
- recommended GTM response
- risk/opportunity framing for leadership

## Example result

### Market signal: category consolidation is increasing buyer demand for interoperability and proof of ROI
- Signal strength: high
- Buyer impact: enterprise buyers are showing more concern about integration cost and implementation complexity
- Recommended response: sharpen messaging around interoperability, operational efficiency, and implementation readiness
- Priority audience: security-conscious mid-market and enterprise buyers with limited internal rollout capacity

### Competitor movement: pricing structure is being simplified to attract smaller accounts
- Likely impact: value sensitivity is increasing in the mid-market segment
- Recommended response: test packaging clarity and value framing in that segment
- Priority: medium; monitor before adjusting pricing strategy

## Recommended prompt

> You are a senior market intelligence strategist. Review the incoming market and competitor signals and identify the true shifts that are likely to influence buyer behavior. Explain why each signal matters, assess its commercial relevance, and recommend a GTM response across messaging, positioning, targeting, and timing.
