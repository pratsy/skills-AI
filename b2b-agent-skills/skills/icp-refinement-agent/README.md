# ICP Refinement Agent

## Why this skill exists

A vague ICP creates broad targeting, weak messaging, and poor conversion efficiency.

This skill helps teams sharpen the ideal customer profile by combining customer evidence, buying patterns, and market reality so GTM teams focus on the highest-probability opportunities.

## Business objective

This skill helps the team answer:

> Which customer segments are most likely to buy, expand, and stay, and which segments should we deprioritize?

## Expert memory layer

High-performing GTM teams build memory around patterns such as:

- which customer types convert consistently
- which traits align with faster sales cycles or higher retention
- where product fit is real versus superficial
- which segments are attractive in theory but poor in execution

This skill turns that pattern recognition into a sharper ICP model and clearer targeting priorities.

## Inputs

- CRM and customer data
- win/loss and retention patterns
- product usage and engagement signals
- current ICP assumptions
- market context and segment trends

## Decision logic

A good ICP refinement should evaluate:

1. buying fit: does the customer have the right problem, urgency, and buying structure?
2. commercial fit: do they carry enough value and expansion potential?
3. operational fit: can the team support them efficiently?
4. segment quality: which groups are consistently stronger performers?
5. strategic fit: what should we deprioritize to protect focus?

Strong ICP refinement is not about adding more labels. It is about making targeting more useful.

## Common failure patterns

- relying on static assumptions instead of real customer evidence
- optimizing for total market size rather than actual conversion probability
- broadening the ICP without segment-level proof
- treating low-fit accounts as targets because they are easy to reach
- ignoring buying triggers and customer engagement patterns

## Outputs

- refined ICP summary
- strongest-fit segments
- buying triggers and structural traits
- deprioritized or low-fit segments
- recommended targeting approach

## Example result

### Refined ICP: mid-market SaaS teams with high operational complexity
- Best-fit segment: 50–500 employee B2B teams with clear workflow inefficiency and scaling pain.
- Common buying trigger: fragmented operations and inability to scale forecasting or pipeline visibility.
- Best message: reduce operating friction and improve decision quality.
- Deprioritize: low-ACV accounts, manual-operations teams with weak expansion potential, and poor product maturity fit.

## Recommended prompt

> You are a senior B2B go-to-market strategist. Analyze the provided customer and market data and refine the ideal customer profile. Identify the most valuable segments, the common buying triggers, and the segments that should be deprioritized. Return a concise ICP summary with commercial and targeting implications.

## Why this is different from a generic prompt

This is not just “rewrite the ICP.”

It is a targeting model that helps teams separate:

- who is actually worth pursuing
- which segments convert better
- which buying triggers signal real fit
- which markets should be deprioritized for focus and efficiency

That is the expert memory that makes segmentation and targeting useful.
